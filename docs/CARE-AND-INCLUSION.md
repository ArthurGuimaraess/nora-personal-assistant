# Care, inclusion and responsible support

## The reason NORA exists

NORA is being developed to make everyday life more manageable for people whose mental health, neurodevelopment, sleep, energy, attention or sensory needs can make planning and routine difficult. The intended audience includes people living with depression, anxiety, ADHD, OCD, insomnia and many other conditions or experiences — and anyone who prefers a calmer, more adaptable way to organize daily life.

The product's purpose is **supportive daily assistance**, not diagnosis or treatment. NORA aims to help with practical friction: deciding what matters today, starting a small task, organizing appointments, keeping a flexible routine, taking a pause, finding a calmer interface and choosing whether to ask for support.

## Design commitments

### 1. No shame, punishment or productivity pressure

- An unfinished task is information, not a moral failure.
- Plans should be easy to adjust when energy, focus or circumstances change.
- Avoid streaks, punitive scores, guilt-based notifications and language that labels someone lazy or unsuccessful.
- Offer small next steps without implying that a person must complete them.

### 2. Different needs, different ways of interacting

- Provide readable layouts, keyboard navigation, adjustable text and button sizes, contrast choices and reduced-stimulation options.
- Keep navigation predictable and avoid forcing voice interaction.
- Make reminders and proactive suggestions optional and adjustable.
- Support people who need fewer choices as well as people who want detailed control.

### 3. Be careful with condition-specific design

- **Depression and low energy:** use gentle language and manageable steps; do not pressure users to maintain a perfect routine.
- **Anxiety:** favor clarity and predictability; do not flood users with alerts or imply that every uncertainty must be resolved immediately.
- **ADHD:** reduce friction around capturing and organizing tasks; allow flexible focus durations and simple prioritization.
- **OCD:** do not reinforce rituals, repeated checking, reassurance loops or compulsive logging. NORA is not an OCD intervention or exposure-and-response-prevention tool.
- **Insomnia:** sleep playlists and routine tools are optional comfort/organization features, not treatment or a replacement for sleep-medicine care.
- **Other conditions:** let the person configure their experience rather than assuming a diagnosis determines their needs.

These are design considerations and goals. They are not claims of clinical benefit, and not every item is implemented in the current prototype.

### 4. User agency and AI transparency

- AI features are optional and must be clearly identified.
- Access to planning data requires user-selected permissions.
- AI-generated actions should be proposals, not silent changes. The person must review and confirm before a proposal alters their plans.
- External AI requests must be explicit; sensitive information should not be sent by default.
- Users should be able to disable optional AI, memory or proactive suggestions.

### 5. Privacy and shared support

- Personal and sensitive records must remain account-scoped.
- Sharing with another person must be explicit, limited and revocable.
- Private reflections should not be automatically forwarded to family, caregivers or external services.
- Public demos must use fictitious data only. Never commit .env, keys, user databases, photos, logs or exports containing personal information.

## What NORA is not

NORA is not a medical device, diagnostic system, therapist, psychiatrist, psychologist, medication advisor or emergency service. It does not diagnose depression, anxiety, ADHD, OCD, insomnia or any other condition. It does not prescribe or change medication, provide psychotherapy, monitor a person continuously or guarantee that an alert/SOS request will reach someone.

A person experiencing a crisis or immediate danger should contact local emergency services or a trusted human/professional support resource. NORA must never be presented as the only source of help.

## Before making stronger mental-health claims

Any future feature that interprets symptoms, recommends interventions, handles crisis risk, tracks treatment or makes clinical claims would require a separate safety, privacy and clinical-governance process, including qualified professional input, user research, risk analysis and appropriate validation. It must not be added merely by changing product copy.

The public repository demonstrates selected software implementation. Its demo does not implement mental-health-specific interventions or clinical workflows.
